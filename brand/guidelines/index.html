Proud Indian · Brand guidelines02 · The idea
02 · The idea

Side by side.

Two figures stand on one baseline. The adult's arm rests across the child's shoulder. It reads as support and company: an Optimist standing alongside a child, which is what our volunteers actually do.

  1. First read: two people. An adult and a child, side by side. This carries the identity.
  2. Second read: π. The two figures and the arm make the shape of pi. It also nods to the Math Challenge.
  3. Third read: P + I. Read as letters, the pair suggests a P and an I: Proud Indian.

The π and P + I readings are a reward for people who notice. We don't lead with them when introducing Proud Indian. The symbol has no face, no hand and no lamp or flame, so it stays clear of political and religious symbols. The old bulb-and-raised-palm logo was retired because a front-facing open palm is an election symbol in India.

Proud Indian · Brand guidelines03 · The symbol
03 · The symbol

Calm geometry, one soft gesture.

The symbol is built from three parts: two circles and one body contour. Everything is straight lines with eased corners, except the arm, which eases down onto the child's shoulder.

  • The child is the hero. The child's head is the only colour in the logo: deep cyan on light grounds.
  • Stand and reach. Stems stand on squared feet; the arm ends round.
  • Serious, with character. Flat colour, no face, no gradients. The playfulness lives in the website's illustrations, not in the logo.
  • Black first. It reads in one colour, so it survives receipts, rubber stamps and embroidery.
"A logo identifies. It rarely describes."Paul Rand, 1991, quoted in our logo research. The meaning of the symbol is earned by the work behind it.

Proud Indian · Brand guidelines05 · Wordmark
05 · The wordmark

Anek Latin, semi-condensed, drawn to match the symbol

Why Anek Latin

It is serious without being cold, stays legible at 24 px, and comes from Ek Type, an Indian foundry, with matching Anek Kannada and Anek Devanagari on the same width and weight axes. No other candidate had a ready Latin, Kannada and Devanagari system from one family.

ಪ್ರೌಡ್ ಇಂಡಿಯನ್प्राउड इंडियन

Companion preview only (Anek Kannada and Anek Devanagari, width 88). Indic logotypes will be drawn and tuned to the same rules when needed.

Drawn from the symbol

  • Width88 upright and slightly condensed, like the figures
  • Weight681 letter stem 9.0 at cap height 40, equal to the symbol's arm
  • Stem endseased r 2.4 the symbol's feet
  • i-dotround, 1.3 × stem rhymes with the heads
  • CaseTitle Case reads as a proper name, an institution
  • Colournever cyan one cyan dot in the system: the child

Spacing and letterspacing

  • The wordmark is outlined artwork. Never retype it in a font, and never track it out.
  • Letters keep the font's own spacing, set as a tight logotype and kerned by area.
  • Word space Proud ⟷ Indian: 18 units, 0.45 × cap.
  • Symbol to wordmark: 0.75 × cap, so the symbol never reads as a third word.
  • From 24 to 40 px use the small master (wm-H-small.svg): weight 659, stems 8.67, more open spacing.

Proud Indian · Brand guidelines12 · Seals
12 · Seals

Seals mark documents and occasions

Geist ring text around the locked symbol. Each seal has colour, one-colour and stamp-production artwork.

Stamp production pi-seal*-stamp-production.svg. One colour, flat vector, heavier inner ring (2.2) because rubber needs it, no filters. Send only these to a stamp maker. Minimum 18 mm.

Screen only pi-seal-80g-SCREEN-ONLY-texture.svg. The ink texture is an SVG filter for mockups and the website. Never print it. A real stamp makes its own texture.

A seal is decoration, not proof of registration. Registration numbers always appear in the document's text.

Proud Indian · Brand guidelines14 · Photography
14 · Photography

Real children, real days, real joy.

  • Real. Our own programmes, from our own albums. Never stock.
  • Consented. Use only photos taken and shared with consent for Proud Indian's use.
  • Child-centred. Prefer children over adult volunteers. Show what children do: paint, perform, learn, play.
  • Joyful, not pitiful. Pride and fun, never need or distress.
  • No watermarks in use. Use clean originals, or crop clear of old watermarks.
  • Cut-out stickers. Cut a child out cleanly, add the paper outline, and let the figure break a frame or stand on a band edge.
  • Protect identities. Crop or blur name badges so no child's full name is readable.

From our albums: a planetarium visit, group dance and rangoli at Kalakriti 3.0, and an art-and-craft session as a cut-out sticker. Older photos are cropped clear of the old watermark.

Proud Indian · Brand guidelines15 · Motion
15 · Motion

Motion that never hides anything

  • The resting state is the finished state. Content is never hidden behind an animation.
  • Cheap to run. Animate only transform, opacity and stroke-dashoffset.
  • Reduced motion respected. prefers-reduced-motion: reduce turns all of it off.
  • Pause control. A visible "Pause motion" toggle in the footer (remembered between visits) stops the loops site-wide and disables autoplaying one-shots, as WCAG 2.2.2 requires.
  • One loop at a time. The sun and the marquee never appear on screen together on phones.

Proud Indian · Brand guidelines16 · Voice and tone
16 · Voice and tone

Short. Warm. Plain English.

  • Short sentences, one idea each. Verbs over abstractions: teach, feed, paint, gather.
  • Warm, not sentimental. Invite people in; never guilt them.
  • Volunteers are Optimists. "Become an Optimist" is the call to join.
  • True, to the number. Stats and registration details stay exact. No per-rupee impact claims.
  • Weekends, not weekly. Sessions are mostly at weekends; never promise "every Saturday".
  • Kalakriti is a festival. A yearly, full day of arts and culture. Never a "workshop".

Rules
  • Status says what can change; Tag says what something is. Status is a soft tint with a dot and a word in five tones. Tag is a grey outline and never carries meaning through colour.
  • Undo, not "Are you sure?" One-click actions that can be reversed save after a few seconds, with Undo in the toast. Deletes, and approvals that need a note or proof, keep a dialog.
  • Dialogs are small and named for the action: 384 px by default, buttons that say the verb (Approve, Delete), never OK.
  • Menus put destructive actions last, in red, after a divider. Details open in a sheet from the right; on phones it becomes a bottom drawer.
  • Toasts sit bottom right and say what happened in a few words.
  • Motion is quick and quiet: 100–200 ms, ease-out, no bounce. Hover effects only on devices that can hover.

Proud Indian · Brand guidelinesA2 · Tables
A2 · Tables

One frame per table.

Most of the dashboard is tables. Each one is a single framed grid, so lists, filters and totals read as one object.

  1. KPI chips above, tinted by status, with a count and the ₹ sum. No stat cards.
  2. View tabs with counts in the frame's top edge for the main status split.
  3. One toolbar line: search, filters written as sentences, Columns, and the page's one primary button.
  4. Column icons say what a column holds. Amounts, dates, counts, IDs and emails use Paper Mono.
  5. Rows are one 36 px line with light column dividers. Long text truncates; the detail sheet has the rest.
  6. A totals row sums money over every filtered row, not just the page.
  7. Group by status for approval queues: collapsible headers with count and total, and Approve or Reject on pending rows.
  8. Rows fill the window without making the page scroll.
